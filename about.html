About

A benchmark you can watch.

JevBench measures decision models by putting them in charge of a real game. The result is a win or a loss, and the footage is there to check.

01 · What we measure

Decisions, not answers.

Most benchmarks grade a model on a single typed answer. JevBench grades it on what happens when it has to act: hundreds of decisions in a row, under time pressure, with a clear outcome at the end.

Jev is a decision model. Instead of writing a reply, it picks one option from a set and reports how confident it is. JevBench ranks Jev alongside open and self-hosted alternatives, and setups that pair a decision model with a guide LLM.

Games come first because they are hard to fake. The next benchmarks cover product decisions such as recovering from failed tool calls and responding to incidents. See the roadmap.

JevBench.dev is independent. It is not affiliated with other benchmarks that use the JevBench name, and every result here comes from a game played on this bench.

02 · Method

How a run is scored.

  1. 01
    The model plays live

    The game's state is read as structured data and the model chooses each move from the legal options.

  2. 02
    The game confirms the win

    StarCraft II: the enemy headquarters is destroyed or critically damaged with Raynor alive, or a person confirms the victory screen. Minecraft: the Ender Dragon is defeated and the exit portal reached.

  3. 03
    Every run is published

    Wins and losses both go on the record, with decisions made, cost and the reason a run ended.

  4. 04
    Evidence is attached

    Stills and video come from the game window only, and are reviewed before they are published.

  5. 05
    Speed and cost are measured

    Latency is timed on every decision of every run. Cost is what the model and its guide were billed; where a provider returns token counts instead of a bill, those tokens are priced at its published rate. A figure marked vendor-reported has not been measured here yet. Published rates: TypeSafe, Google, Laya-MLX.
